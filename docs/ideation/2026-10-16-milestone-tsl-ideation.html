The Core Architectural Paradox: Why Setting SL = TP1 at Touch Fails

The user proposed ratcheting the Stop Loss to Take Profit 1 (TP1) the moment price hits TP1, following the concept of a Trailing Stop Loss (TSL). While conceptually intuitive, applying this naively on 3x daily leveraged ETPs (e.g. QQQ3l_EQ, 3TSMl_EQ) causes an immediate self-liquidation bug due to market microstructure and codebase execution ordering:

1. Zero-Distance Execution Loop

In position_monitor.py:405-410:
405: active_stop = max(pos.stop_loss, pos.trailing_stop_loss)
406: if current_price <= active_stop:
407:     self._handle_stop_exit(pos, current_price)
If stop_loss = tp1 when price touches TP1, distance is $0.00. On the very next tick loop, any zero or negative tick increment instantly liquidates the remaining 67% shares.

2. Roll (1984) Bid-Ask Bounce on 3x ETPs

European 3x ETPs have typical LSE spreads of 15–45 bps. Touching the Ask at TP1 means the resting Bid is already lower than TP1. A stop placed at TP1 will instantly fill on the bid side. Furthermore, Trading 212 API throws an HTTP 400 Invalid Payload when submitting a resting stop order within the broker's minimum tick distance of market price.

Codebase Precedent: Why the System Moves to Break-Even at TP1, Not TP1

The current codebase (position_monitor.py:477-480) moves the stop to Break-Even (Entry Price) at TP1, and then ratchets to TP1 only at TP2 (+2.0R) (line 513-514). This guarantees an invariant 1.0R volatility buffer between the active price and the protective stop at every milestone.
